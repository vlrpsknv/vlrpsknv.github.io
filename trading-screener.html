Starting point

Digash is a real-time crypto screener with strong data and a loyal audience. Only 3% of the people who registered went on to pay. I redesigned the product first, then invented its main feature, the Depth Map.

Two layouts I rejected

Beginners could not read the order book, and professionals would not give up precision. I had the same raw liquidity data as Bookmap or TradingLite and did not want to copy their complexity.

The team proposed a 3×3 grid and a wave shape. I tested both and dropped them: they wasted space and the clusters overlapped. My first prototype was a scatter plot, distance from price against volume. It collapsed in the center, and it gave me the idea to keep: distance is the anchor.

The Depth Map

I turned the scatter plot into a radar. Each bubble is a cluster of large resting orders. Its distance from the center is the distance from the price, its size is the volume, its color is buy or sell.

For professionals I added a sortable depth widget that works like a classic order book. The Depth Map became the feature users mentioned most, and revenue doubled after launch.

The redesign that came first

Before the Depth Map I redesigned the whole product: the core screens, the filters, a notification center, simpler settings and pricing, a mobile layout. Registration-to-subscription conversion went from 3% to 14% before the Depth Map existed.
